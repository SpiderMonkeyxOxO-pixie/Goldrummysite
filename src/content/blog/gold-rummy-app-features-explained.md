## The Three Core Rummy Formats

Indian Rummy apps typically build around three well-established formats, and Gold Rummy follows the same structure:

**Points Rummy** is the fastest format. A single deal is played, and at the end, points are tallied based on the cards left in each losing player's hand. It's the format most people play when they want a quick, self-contained game rather than a longer session.

**Pool Rummy** stretches across multiple deals. Players accumulate points as they lose hands, and once a player's total crosses a fixed limit — usually 101 or 201 points, depending on the pool variant — they're eliminated. The last player standing takes the pool. This format rewards consistency over the course of several hands rather than a single lucky one.

**Deals Rummy** plays out over a fixed, pre-agreed number of deals. Whoever has accumulated the fewest points once every deal is complete wins the prize pool. It's a good middle ground for players who want a defined start and end point to a session.

Knowing which format you're joining before you sit down at a table matters — the strategy for a single Points hand is quite different from managing your position across a full Pool Rummy game.

## Built for a Phone Screen

A lot of early real-money card apps were essentially desktop interfaces squeezed onto mobile. Gold Rummy, like most current-generation Rummy apps, is built the other way around: card selection, arranging your hand into sequences and sets, and declaring are all designed around tap and drag gestures sized for a thumb rather than a mouse cursor. That matters more than it sounds — misreading a card during a real-money hand because a tap target was too small is a genuinely frustrating way to lose.
